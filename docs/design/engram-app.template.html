One app, three screens

The wall from 3, the detail page and episode grid from 2, the next-up hero and the gone-from-disk state from 1 — on a new palette and typeface, and rebuilt around the thing you flagged: a record you can write to by hand, for everything you watched before Plex was keeping score.

SYSTEM

Palette and type

Espresso rather than slate, so artwork is the only saturated thing on screen. Jade is the affirmative accent and is deliberately not gold, so "finished" never collides with "in progress". Archivo names every title and every control; Martian Mono sets every figure, date, count and external id, and nothing else. Splitting the two families by job rather than by size is what keeps a count from reading like a label — and it keeps the mono out of the one place it hurts, which is a show name under a 118px tile.

Add watched

The screen your note was missing. Nothing here requires the title to be on disk, in Sonarr, or in Plex at all — it searches TMDB, writes a permanent title, and marks whole seasons in one go. TMDB returns poster_path inline with each search result and its image CDN needs no key, so results normally arrive with real artwork; the generated covers here are the fallback for a title TMDB has no poster for.

2019

Precision is stored with the event, so the record can say “2019” instead of pretending you watched 62 episodes on 1 January.

writes 54 episodes · source manual · precision year · presence not on disk

Why it is shaped like this

Season-level checkboxes, not 62 rows. Backfilling a decade of television one episode at a time is how a feature like this quietly never gets used.

What it costs you first

A TMDB key, which is currently blank in .env. Nothing on this screen works without one — it is the only search that can find a show you do not own.

What this changes underneath

watch_event.watched_at is notNull(), and manual backfill cannot honour that. "Breaking Bad, sometime around 2019" has no timestamp. Two ways out, and the second is better: make the column nullable and teach watch_state to ignore nulls, or keep it non-null and add a watched_precision column — exact | day | month | year | unknown — where the timestamp is a representative instant and precision says how much of it to believe. The view keeps working, sorting stays sane, and the UI renders "2019" rather than a fake January date.

Three smaller consequences, all of which the schema already absorbs: manual rows are just another source, so UNIQUE (source, source_event_id) still makes re-entry idempotent with a synthetic id; raw is notNull() and would hold the form input rather than a payload; and a title that was never on disk simply has no library_presence row, which is already distinct from one that had files and lost them.

Promote

Manual entry from "unglamorous but worth doing early" in the idea pool to a core write path, alongside ingest.

Decide

Nullable watched_at versus a precision column, before any manual-entry code is written.

Unblock

Fill in TMDB_API_KEY. It gates both this screen and the enrichment queue.

Reframe

The notes describe Engram as durable watch history. It is a record you keep, and Plex is one of the things that writes to it.

Artwork is your own, pulled from the Plex server and embedded. Breaking Bad is an illustrative search result, not something in your record. Buttons do nothing.
